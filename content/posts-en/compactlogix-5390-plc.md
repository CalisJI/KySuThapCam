On October 29, 2026, Control.com reported that **Rockwell Automation introduced the CompactLogix 5390**, a PLC aimed at machine builders and system integrators. According to the manufacturer's specifications cited in the report, its footprint is **43% smaller** than the CompactLogix 5380 while expanding motion-control and connectivity capabilities.

## Key specifications

Depending on the selected controller model, the 5390 family supports:

- Up to **128 motion axes**.
- Up to **32 local PointMax I/O modules**.
- Up to **300 EtherNet/IP nodes** and **4,000 OPC UA nodes**.
- Integrated safety features, **CIP Security**, Syslog and capabilities described as aligned with **IEC 62443-4-2**.

These are **upper-end configuration limits**, not specifications guaranteed for every model. Control.com notes that entry-level configurations support around eight motion axes, 60 EtherNet/IP nodes and 300 OPC UA nodes.

## What makes this relevant?

An industrial controller increasingly needs to coordinate drives, distributed I/O, HMIs and plant-level data platforms, not merely run ladder logic. OPC UA support can help with interoperability across IIoT, SCADA and manufacturing information systems.

The controller is designed to work with Rockwell's **Kinetix, PowerFlex, Stratix** and **FactoryTalk Design Studio** ecosystem, making it potentially attractive to sites already using Logix-based automation.

## Relevance for Vietnamese manufacturers

A basic machine with modest I/O requirements may not justify such a controller. However, multi-axis machines and connected production lines may benefit from reviewing its capabilities.

Engineers should verify the **exact CPU model, supported axes and nodes, licenses, software compatibility, scan-time requirements and total system costs** before choosing a configuration.
